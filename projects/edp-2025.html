Overview

The IEEE Student Day UAE Engineering Design Project required teams to build a catapult with no electronics whatsoever — purely mechanical. Projectiles were two different sized balls, and scoring was based on accuracy: the closer to the center of a target at varying distances, the more points.

I designed all the frame and the 3D printed parts in Fusion 360 and built them on my printer. The wooden frame and final assembly were handled by the rest of the team. We placed 2nd overall.

The two custom-designed key mechanisms were an adjustable arm stop to control launch angle and a dual-profile ball holder that accommodates both ball sizes without swapping parts.

3D-Printed Components

Three custom parts were designed and 3D printed. Each solves a specific mechanical problem.

Adjustable Arm Stop Limits how far back the throwing arm can be pulled, controlling launch angle and therefore range. Sliding adjustment lets the team dial in distance without rebuilding.
Dual-Profile Ball Holder One part handles both ball sizes. The outer cup fits the large ball; an inset profile inside it seats the smaller ball centrally.
Rope Guides Printed brackets that keep the elastic launch rope aligned along the arm, preventing lateral slip and ensuring consistent release angle every throw.
