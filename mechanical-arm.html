Mechanical Arm Design

📅 2023 ⏱️ 6 months CAD + manufacturing 🛠️ Blender, Fusion 360, 3D Printing

Overview

A fully-designed, 3D-printed robotic manipulator arm with 6 degrees of freedom. This project combines mechanical design principles with digital fabrication techniques, resulting in a functional robot that can pick, place, and manipulate objects with precision.

The design started as CAD sketches, evolved into detailed 3D models, and was optimized for 3D printing without sacrificing strength or precision. The final arm is assembled from ~200 individual printed parts.

Design Goals

I set out to create a robot that would be:

  • Affordable: ~$400 in materials vs $10k+ for commercial arms
  • Modular: Easy to repair and upgrade individual joints
  • Precise: Sub-millimeter repeatability for fine manipulation
  • Printable: Designed specifically for FDM 3D printing constraints
  • Functional: Actually work and complete real tasks

Design Process

Phase 1: Research & Concepts

Studied existing robotic arms, analyzed kinematics, and sketched 10+ different design approaches. Decided on a 6-DOF design with sequential revolute joints.

Phase 2: CAD Modeling

Built the complete assembly in Fusion 360 with:

  • Detailed motor mounts and servo brackets
  • Rotating joints with tight tolerances
  • Cable routing and management systems
  • Stress analysis to ensure no parts would break

Phase 3: Print Optimization

Optimized every part for 3D printing: Added support structures where needed, oriented parts for strength, used infill patterns strategically, and tested critical joints with multiple materials.

Phase 4: Assembly & Testing

Printed, post-processed (sanding, vapor smoothing), and assembled all parts. Tested each joint individually, then the complete arm under various loads.

Technical Specifications

Dimensions & Payload

Reach 60cm (fully extended)
Payload 2kg at full extension
Repeatability ±0.5mm
Weight 8kg total
Speed ~30°/second per joint
DOF 6 (full articulation)

Materials

Structural Parts: PETG 3D-printed (100% infill at critical joints)

Bearings: Ceramic ball bearings (low friction)

Actuators: MG996R servo motors (6x)

Controller: Arduino Mega + custom servo controller PCB

Power: 5V/20A power supply

Manufacturing Stats

Total Print Time: ~400 hours

Filament Used: 6.2 kg PETG

Number of Parts: 207 individual components

Post-Processing: ~60 hours sanding, cleaning, and assembly

Cost: $380 in materials

Challenges & Solutions

Challenge 1: Joint Durability

Problem: Early iterations had joints that wore out quickly under repeated stress.

Solution: Switched from printed bushings to ceramic ball bearings and redesigned the joint geometry to distribute forces more evenly. Added a second bearing at the far end of each joint for support.

Challenge 2: Print Orientation

Problem: Parts printed in wrong orientation broke easily.

Solution: Ran stress simulations for each part and oriented them so the load was perpendicular to the print layers. Tested multiple infill patterns to find the best strength-to-weight ratio.

Challenge 3: Servo Synchronization

Problem: Getting 6 servo motors to move smoothly and coordinate complex motions.

Solution: Built a custom PID controller in Arduino firmware with feedback from potentiometers at each joint for closed-loop control.

Current Applications

The arm is currently being used for:

  • Object sorting and pick-and-place tasks
  • 3D art installations and kinetic sculptures
  • Educational demonstrations on robotics
  • Testing and iteration for v2.0 design

Key Learnings

  • Design for manufacturing: A great design on paper means nothing if it can't be built
  • Iterate rapidly: Print and test early, fail fast, improve constantly
  • Tolerance stacking: Tiny errors in each part add up to big problems in assemblies
  • Material properties matter: Understanding PETG's anisotropy was crucial
  • Over-engineer critical parts: Bearings, joints, and motor mounts are worth the extra care

Version 2.0 Plans

Based on this experience, I'm planning improvements for the next iteration:

  • Lighter design with carbon fiber inserts in critical areas
  • Increased payload capacity to 5kg
  • Add gripper with force feedback for delicate tasks
  • Integrate ROS (Robot Operating System) for advanced autonomy
  • Open-source the design for community improvements
